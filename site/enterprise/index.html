kitshn for enterprise

Every internal app, one map of who gets in.

kitshn deploys your company's sites, Workers and containers from GitHub to your own Cloudflare account and servers. One map decides which teams, partners and machines can open each one, and Cloudflare Access enforces it.

Access

Teams, not passwords

A circle is a group: an email, a company domain such as @acme.com, a device, a machine, an IP range, or everyone. A circle can ask that a person match all of its rules, for example your domain and the office network.

  • A circle can ask for a passkey after the login.
  • Each share has a level, and a share for a contractor can end on a date.
  • People sign in through Cloudflare Access. kitshn stores no passwords.
Deploys

One pipeline for every kind of app

A recipe in each repo, kitshn.config.ts, says what the repo deploys. A push to main deploys production, and each pull request gets its own preview address that goes away when it closes.

Security

No secrets in the repo, no open ports

GitHub signs each run with an OIDC token, and kitshn trusts only the GitHub accounts on its list. It reads the recipe as data and never runs it. Servers talk through Cloudflare tunnels, so no port is open to the internet.

Control

Know who changed what, and stop it fast

  1. History of every change

    Each share, move and deploy is one revision with who made it. You undo your own newest change in one click. History keeps up to 90 days.

  2. An emergency pause

    A big red button closes every share at once, or every share but the public ones, and keeps the owners in. Resume needs a fresh passkey login.

  3. Agents that ask first

    Coding agents on your machines act through their machine. Each right is off, automatic, or asks a circle of approvers before it runs.

Start
